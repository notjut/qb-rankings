How the rankings work

  1. 1One score per game

    50 is an average game. Every 10 points is one big step better or worse than average. 70 is excellent. 80 is historic.

  2. 2Nine stats count

    Turnovers and yards per attempt matter most. Then touchdown passes, passing yards, the defense he faced, then completion rate, QBR, rushing and sacks taken.

  3. 3Judged against its own season

    Each game is compared with every other game that same year, so 300 yards in 2003 is worth more than 300 yards today.

  4. 4The situation counts

    Winning adds 2 points and losing takes 2 away. A road game adds 1 and a home game takes 1 away. A game-winning drive adds 2. Freezing cold, heavy wind, rain or snow add up to 4. Playing without a regular top target adds up to 3.

  5. 5Running can only help

    A big rushing day counts. A quiet one does not hurt. Kneel-downs at the end of a win are ignored.

  6. 6Real games only

    The quarterback has to play all four quarters. Leave hurt and it does not count. A starter benched for bad play still counts, because he earned it.

  7. 7Tap any game

    See what pushed it up, what held it back, or put two games side by side.
